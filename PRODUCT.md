# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary audience is UK and EMEA suppliers choosing an EDI platform to connect with retailers. They need to begin exchanging documents without acquiring specialist EDI knowledge, committing to a lengthy implementation, or accepting an inflexible enterprise contract.

Larger suppliers and retailers are a secondary audience for the platform's enterprise capabilities. Technical and integration teams are a further audience for the developer experience, APIs, integration platform, A2A support, agentic capabilities, and MCP access.

## Product Purpose

EDI Spark makes retailer-supplier EDI effortless. It provides an approachable managed interface alongside a comprehensive integration platform that parses and maps business documents into UBL, connects suppliers and retailers, and supports API-driven, A2A, agentic, and MCP-enabled workflows.

Success means a supplier can understand the offer quickly, connect to the retailers and software they already use, and begin a free trial with confidence that the platform can grow with them.

## Positioning

EDI Spark is the simplest route into retailer EDI: quick to adopt without prior EDI expertise, setup fees, annual contracts, or opaque pricing, while remaining technically capable enough for enterprise and developer-led integrations.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: Start a free 30-day trial.
- Secondary CTA: Book a demo.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: Effortless EDI. Pay only for what you exchange.
- Belief ladder: EDI Spark connects suppliers to the retailers and SaaS tools they use; setup is quick and does not require EDI expertise; pricing is transparent, flexible, and low-risk; the platform is technically serious, with UBL mapping, APIs, A2A, agentic workflows, and MCP; and it scales from smaller suppliers to enterprise operations.
- Proof on hand: Connections to hundreds of trading partners, including every major UK retailer; integration with more than 300 common SaaS products; and demonstrable API, A2A, agentic, and MCP capabilities. As a new startup, EDI Spark should substantiate claims through visible product and technical evidence rather than imply an established customer history.

## Brand Personality

Approachable, capable, and trustworthy. The experience should first create relief by making EDI feel simple, then build confidence in the platform's reliability and depth. Language should be direct and understandable to suppliers while giving technical evaluators a clear path to substantive detail.

Reference points are Stedi for API and EDI credibility, Shopify UK for approachable commercial language, and Stripe for technical product storytelling and presentation of complex infrastructure.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble dated enterprise software or jargon-heavy EDI websites. Avoid generic AI/SaaS styling, secretive pricing, and any presentation that suggests long setup times or consultant-led implementation. OpenText and TrueCommerce are explicit anti-references for the website's tone and experience.

## Design Principles

1. Make complexity feel handled. Explain outcomes first and reveal technical depth when it earns confidence.
2. Show, do not merely claim. Use product evidence, real integration coverage, and concrete technical artifacts to establish credibility.
3. Remove the traditional EDI tax. Keep pricing, setup expectations, and next steps plain, visible, and low-risk.
4. Serve two depths without splitting the brand. Keep the main journey approachable for suppliers while providing serious detail for enterprise and developer evaluators.
5. Earn trust through precision. Prefer specific, supportable claims over inflated startup language or vague AI promises.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the public website. Support full keyboard operation, visible focus states, semantic screen-reader structure, sufficient colour contrast, and reduced-motion preferences as baseline requirements.
