# Design System: EDI Spark

## 1. Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Clear Exchange"**

EDI Spark should feel like a brightly lit logistics control room after the paperwork has disappeared: calm, legible, and quietly powerful, with every connection understandable at a glance. The system pairs an approachable human voice with visible technical capability. It should make suppliers feel immediate relief, then reward deeper inspection with the precision expected by enterprise and developer audiences.

The visual identity uses committed indigo as infrastructure rather than decoration. Clear information flow, product evidence, and purposeful interaction carry the story. Responsive motion confirms that the system is active and connected without turning routine product explanation into theatre.

This system explicitly rejects the dated enterprise-software look of OpenText and TrueCommerce, jargon-heavy EDI sites, generic AI/SaaS styling, secretive pricing, and any visual suggestion of long or consultant-led setup.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Committed indigo balanced by true neutrals and a clearly distinct accent
- Humanist, highly legible typography with a capable but approachable voice
- Product and integration evidence presented as the primary visual material
- Responsive motion that explains connection, mapping, and exchange
- Transparent commercial information treated as part of the product experience

## 2. Colors

The palette is anchored by a deep violet-indigo that feels assured and infrastructural, not neon, mystical, or generically "AI".

### Primary

- **Exchange Indigo** ([to be resolved during implementation]): Carries 30–60% of key brand surfaces, including the hero environment, major calls to action, and moments that represent the exchange network. Its final value must remain within the approved violet-indigo family and support accessible text contrast.

### Secondary

- **Signal Accent** ([to be resolved during implementation]): A distinct hue and lightness used to show successful connections, active routes, and selected interaction states. It must not be another purple variant.

### Neutral

- **Clear Field** ([to be resolved during implementation]): A true neutral light surface for dense explanations, pricing, documentation pathways, and areas where comprehension must lead.
- **Technical Ink** ([to be resolved during implementation]): High-contrast text with only a trace of the primary hue, targeting at least 7:1 contrast against the main background.
- **Quiet Surface** ([to be resolved during implementation]): A subtle tonal layer for grouping related content without relying on floating cards.

### Named Rules

**The Committed Indigo Rule.** Indigo must carry 30–60% of key brand surfaces. Do not reduce it to a timid accent scattered across an otherwise generic SaaS page.

**The Infrastructure, Not AI Rule.** Indigo represents dependable exchange infrastructure. Purple gradients, neon glows, cosmic imagery, and synthetic AI symbolism are prohibited.

**The Legibility Rule.** Final colour tokens must meet WCAG 2.2 AA, with body text targeting at least 7:1 contrast on primary reading surfaces.

**Character:** The type system should feel open, practical, and unmistakably competent. Humanist forms keep supplier-facing language approachable, while disciplined weights, spacing, and numerical treatment preserve enterprise credibility.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** ([to be resolved], fluid scale with a maximum no larger than 6rem, line-height approximately 1): Short, direct homepage claims only; balanced wrapping and letter spacing no tighter than -0.04em are mandatory.
- **Headline** ([to be resolved], fluid scale, compact line-height): Section arguments and major product capabilities.
- **Title** ([to be resolved], medium or semibold): Named integrations, workflow steps, developer resources, and pricing concepts.
- **Body** ([to be resolved], regular, generous line-height): Explanations in plain English, capped at 65–75 characters per line.
- **Label** ([to be resolved], medium): Navigation, form labels, metadata, and short interface states. Sentence case is the default.

### Named Rules

**The Plain-English Hierarchy Rule.** Type must clarify the decision path: outcome first, evidence second, implementation detail on demand. Never simulate authority with dense jargon or walls of small text.

**The One Human Voice Rule.** Use one deliberately chosen humanist sans family unless implementation proves that a second family performs a necessary role. Monospace is reserved for genuine code and protocol data, never as decorative shorthand for technical credibility.

## 4. Elevation

The system is flat by default. Depth comes from committed colour fields, spacing, scale, and clearly nested information rather than decorative shadows or glass effects. A compact structural shadow may be introduced for functional overlays or transient navigation during implementation; static content should remain grounded in the page.

### Named Rules

**The Grounded Surface Rule.** Marketing sections and product evidence stay flat at rest. Shadows are reserved for an interaction that has genuinely moved above the page, such as a menu, dialog, or selected draggable object.

## 6. Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** make exchange tangible through real UI, integration marks, document transformations, code, and clear connection flows.
- **Do** let committed indigo carry the identity across 30–60% of key brand surfaces.
- **Do** use responsive motion for immediate feedback and intelligible transitions, with a reduced-motion alternative for every effect.
- **Do** place pricing, trial terms, setup expectations, and proof close to the claims they support.
- **Do** maintain WCAG 2.2 AA keyboard, focus, semantic, contrast, and reduced-motion requirements from the first implementation.

### Don't:

- **Don't** reproduce the dated enterprise-software look associated with OpenText or TrueCommerce.
- **Don't** build a jargon-heavy EDI site that makes suppliers learn the implementation before they understand the outcome.
- **Don't** use generic AI/SaaS styling: purple gradients, neon glows, glass cards, decorative network orbs, and interchangeable rounded-card grids are forbidden.
- **Don't** hide pricing or visually imply long setup times, annual-contract negotiation, or consultant-led implementation.
- **Don't** use product-free decoration where real integration coverage, interface evidence, UBL transformations, API examples, A2A flows, or MCP capabilities can establish trust.
- **Don't** repeat tiny uppercase eyebrows, numbered section markers, gradient text, side-stripe accents, or decorative grid backgrounds as page scaffolding.
